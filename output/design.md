# Design (Problem 10)

The idea: **a campus sunrise, hosted by Professor Dan.** Handsome Dan, Yale's bulldog,
reimagined as a sweet elderly professor, welcomes shoppers like it's office hours.
Still navy and white, now with a sunrise gold.

| What changed | Why it should help customers stick around and buy |
|---|---|
| **Fonts:** Fraunces, a soft old-style serif, for headings; Graduate, a varsity block font, for labels, the marquee bar and stamps; Inter for body text. All three are bundled with the site, so nothing loads from other servers. | It reads "college bookstore", not "generic template", and that feels official and trustworthy. Clear body text keeps prices and details easy to read. |
| **Color:** navy and white stay the base, with sunrise gold (`#f2c14e`) only on the main button, the shopper's chat bubbles and small accents. | One warm accent tells the eye where to click ("Shop all products"), without breaking the Yale look. |
| **Hero:** a dawn sky with twinkling stars and a rising sun over a Gothic campus skyline (inline SVG). Professor Dan sits on the rooftops with a "Boola Boola" pennant and an "Ask me!" bubble. | The first second says *Yale, New Haven, warm*. It's an emotional hook, and it leads straight to the two actions that matter: shop, or ask Dan. |
| **Hierarchy:** a small varsity "kicker" line above every section heading. Garment icons on the "Shop by style" tiles. A navy band for the collections, with numbered cards. | Shoppers can scan the page in seconds and jump to their shelf. The navy band breaks up the page so the collections stand out. |
| **Product cards:** a tilted varsity stamp naming the shelf, a navy price tag that swings on hover, size letters with sold-out sizes crossed out (your size ringed), and the photo zooming slightly on hover. | Price and availability are visible before a click, so there are fewer dead-end clicks on sold-out items. The card feels like a tag on a real rack. |
| **Single-item page:** breadcrumbs, a photo that zooms where you point, color swatches instead of color words, and an **Ask Professor Dan about this** button. | Shoppers can inspect lettering up close (the "is it embroidered?" moment) and get help without leaving the item, which removes the last doubts before buying. |
| **Motion:** a slow marquee of shop facts, page text rising in, cards rising into view as you scroll, a shimmer while products load, and a gentle wiggle on the chat button. All of it stops for people who've asked their device for less motion. | The site feels alive and quick: loading looks intentional rather than broken, and the moving bits draw the eye to the chat and the products. |
| **Professor Dan** is drawn in HTML/CSS, not an image: a cream bulldog with a tan eye patch, bushy white brows, round tortoiseshell glasses, an underbite, a mortarboard with a swinging tassel and a Yale-blue bow tie. He blinks. He tilts and "thinks" while answering. Tap him and he adjusts his glasses. | A friendly face makes asking for help feel easy. Shoppers who chat find the right size and item more often, and a mascot people like is a reason to come back. |
| **Chat: a blue sunrise.** The messages sit on a sky that rises from navy to dawn gold, with a sun that rises when the chat opens and drifting clouds. Dan's replies are on ruled index cards; your messages are in gold bubbles. His typing line reads "Professor Dan is consulting his notes". The chat button is Dan's face with "Ask Professor Dan". | The chat feels like a small, calming moment instead of a support widget, so people use it more. And the more they use it, the more they find (and buy) things that fit. |
| **Voice:** `prompts/prompt.md` makes the agent Professor Dan: warm and gently professorial ("Ah, splendid question."). At most one flourish per reply, and none on "sold out" answers. Every honesty and safety rule is unchanged. | Charm without losing clarity: prices and stock are still stated plainly, so customers keep trusting the answers. |
| **Small delights:** a shield-shaped "CC" crest that tilts on hover, the footer's skyline with Dan signing off "Boola Boola!", and a 404 page where Dan "looked in every stack" of the library. | Even mistakes and edges feel cared for, which keeps people browsing instead of bouncing. |
